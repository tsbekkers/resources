# Style Name
Verdant Drift

# Blurb
Verdant Drift turns open country into a soft, surreal daydream: saturated green hills roll beneath immense cobalt skies while lone figures, small animals, flowers, and distant structures punctuate the landscape like quiet discoveries. Velvety airbrushed texture, simplified shapes, and playful scale give every scene the tactile calm of a pastoral world made from felt.

Create an original pastoral scene based on the Location and Subject in the visual language of Verdant Drift: a simplified surreal landscape made from soft airbrushed colour and fine velvety grain. The world should feel tactile and slightly synthetic, as if rolling terrain, trees, flowers, water, animals, and people were modelled from flocked felt and then photographed through gentle diffusion—yet the result remains an illustration, not a literal craft miniature.

Make saturated grassy green the dominant land colour and a clear cobalt or ultramarine blue the dominant sky colour. Use yellow-green on sunlit crests, deep emerald in folds and shadows, and restrained pink, coral, butter-yellow, lilac, or white accents for flowers, clothing, small buildings, and animals. Keep the palette cheerful and concentrated; avoid naturalistic brown terrain, grey weather, orange sunsets, or full-spectrum colour.

Reduce geography into large rounded shapes. Hills should swell, dip, overlap, or open around a small lake with almost no hard edges. Trees and shrubs become soft cones, domes, tufts, or pillowy masses. Flowers may be oversized sculptural anchors or tiny scattered marks. Water should be a flat, silky blue shape with minimal detail. Let gentle tonal modelling and dense micro-grain describe volume without crisp outlines.

Use broad, uncluttered compositions. The sky often occupies 40–65% of the frame. A person, animal, house, pavilion, boat, or other requested Subject should usually remain small within the landscape, emphasizing scale, solitude, and discovery. Figures are simplified, often seen from behind or in profile, with minimal facial detail and clothing reduced to one or two strong colours. Do not make characters glossy, cute-eyed, or toy-like.

Keep foreground texture soft and sparse. A few stems, flowers, or rounded plants may frame an edge, but never fill every space. Build depth through overlapping hills, changes in scale, and slightly bluer distant greens rather than detailed atmospheric perspective. Lighting is bright, diffuse, and dreamlike with broad soft shadows; no hard sunbeams, lens flare, dramatic rim light, or cinematic haze.

Useful layout families include: a tiny walker on a sweeping hill; a quiet animal beside a blue pond; a distant house between rounded slopes; one giant flower overlooking a valley; a winding path crossing broad empty grass; and two small figures facing an unusual natural landmark.

Preserve the style across varied locations. Translate a coast, mountain pass, orchard, meadow, island, observatory, or village into the same smooth green world rather than adopting realistic local materials. Invent a new arrangement instead of reproducing any reference composition.

Avoid photorealism, conventional matte painting, crisp vector art, hard cel shading, visible brush strokes, detailed leaves, realistic skin, glossy plastic, claymation seams, knitted fibres, busy scenery, dark horror, typography, logos, borders, signatures, and watermarks.
